Develop a configurable hospital layout and mapping feature for the healthcare ticketing system. Each hospital must have its own layout because hospitals may contain different buildings, wings, departments, floor plans, and numbers of floors.

Create a hierarchical structure that allows administrators to configure:

Hospital
Building or site
Wing or zone
Floor or level
Department
Corridor or shared area
Individual room or facility

Each room or area should have a unique identifier and be linked to its hospital, building, floor, department, and map coordinates. Administrators must be able to add, rename, move, deactivate, or remove locations without changing the main application code.

Support a comprehensive and expandable list of hospital room and area types, including:

Patient bedrooms, private rooms, shared wards, isolation rooms and negative-pressure rooms
Accident and emergency areas, triage rooms, resuscitation bays, treatment bays and observation units
Intensive care, high-dependency, neonatal intensive care and cardiac care units
Operating theatres, anaesthetic rooms, recovery rooms, scrub areas and sterile preparation rooms
Consultation, examination, treatment, procedure and minor surgery rooms
Imaging rooms, including X-ray, CT, MRI, ultrasound, mammography and nuclear medicine
Laboratories, pathology rooms, blood banks, specimen collection rooms and mortuaries
Pharmacies, medication rooms and controlled-drug storage areas
Maternity wards, delivery rooms, birthing suites and postnatal units
Paediatric, neonatal and specialist treatment rooms
Mental health assessment rooms, therapy rooms and secure patient areas
Rehabilitation, physiotherapy, occupational therapy and hydrotherapy rooms
Dental, ophthalmology, audiology and other specialist clinical rooms
Staff offices, meeting rooms, training rooms, changing rooms and break rooms
Reception areas, waiting rooms, information desks and visitor facilities
Kitchens, cafeterias, storage rooms, linen rooms, laundry rooms and waste-disposal areas
Cleaning cupboards, utility rooms, sluice rooms and decontamination areas
Plant rooms, electrical rooms, server rooms, maintenance workshops and equipment stores
Toilets, accessible toilets, showers, lifts, stairwells, entrances, exits and fire-escape routes
Corridors, lobbies, car parks, loading bays, gardens and other shared or external areas

The room catalogue must remain configurable so that hospitals can add specialist or locally named rooms that are not included in the default list.

Allow every submitted ticket to be assigned to a precise location. Users should be able to select the hospital, building, floor, department and room when reporting an issue. Where appropriate, they should also be able to select the affected location directly from an interactive floor plan.

Use the configured layouts to generate an interactive heatmap showing where issues are occurring. Users should be able to switch between hospitals, buildings and floors and filter the heatmap by:

Ticket priority or severity
Issue category
Ticket status
Department or room type
Assigned team
Date or reporting period
Number of issues in an area
Average response or resolution time
Resolved, unresolved, overdue or recurring issues

Use a clear visual system. For example, colour can represent priority, intensity can represent the number of tickets, and icons can identify issue categories or ticket statuses. Include a visible legend so that users can understand the map without relying on colour alone.

Selecting a room or heatmap area should display a summary containing the room name, department, number of active tickets, highest-priority issue, overdue tickets and recent issue history. Authorised users should then be able to open the related tickets.

When several tickets exist in the same or nearby location, group them into a hotspot instead of displaying overlapping markers. The heatmap should update when tickets are created, resolved, reprioritised or moved to another location.

The layout must support both desktop and mobile devices. It should also accommodate accessibility requirements, including keyboard navigation, readable labels, sufficient colour contrast and patterns or symbols for users who cannot distinguish heatmap colours.

Protect sensitive healthcare information by displaying operational ticket data only. Do not expose patient information on the floor plan or heatmap. Access to detailed tickets and restricted areas must follow the user’s role and permissions.

The completed feature should allow every hospital to maintain an accurate, individualised floor layout while giving authorised teams a clear visual overview of the location, volume, priority and status of reported issues.
